Voice & visuals

From Jelani's brand interview. Read this before you write or design anything.

How it sounds

  • Jelani speaks as "I". It's a founder-run brand.
  • A thoughtful journal entry meets intelligent editorial. Short, but a little dreamy.
  • Facts stated plainly: what's on, how much, until when.
  • Scarcity only when it's true: limited run, limited numbers.
  • Ads can be bold. The site and organic posts stay understated. No emojis on the website.
  • "Angels" only once: the last-call email. The VIP window is called First Pick.

How it looks

  • A page from her scrapbook: grainy, motion-blurred film, taped-in prints, vellum overlays, star stickers, a ticket stub.
  • Three type voices: handwriting for the name and notes, typewriter for the facts (dates, %, code), a small editorial serif for headlines.
  • Pieces in her life, not on a beach: windswept grass, flowers up close, real rooms, candid laughter.
  • Candid over perfect. Mix campaign shots, grainy film and iPhone snaps.
  • Break up product shots with text frames and close-ups (the 444 embroidery, print details).
  • Soft paper off-white, dusty pink, plum, moss and apricot. One hand-drawn accent line in acid yellow or red. No black.
  • Signature ideas: the First Pick ticket stub (ADMIT ONE · WED 25.11 · 7PM), a typewriter data-sheet of the offer, the $500 wardrobe as a scrapbook spread, "Black Friday, gently" as a ring-bound zine.

Hard rules

  • Never mention the founding story or any cause in sale content.
  • Say "recycled" only about products that are. Use "crafted from high-quality recycled materials".
  • Product names in Title Case: Scout, Isla, Darcy, Camo Hat.
  • Lead with in-stock styles (Scout, Camo Hat) unless a restock lands.
